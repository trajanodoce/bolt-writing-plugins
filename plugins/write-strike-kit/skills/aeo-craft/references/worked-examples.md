# Worked examples — every pass, from real Bolt.new pieces (Aug 2026)

Real before/afters from the SMB campaign and the prompt-queueing launch. These are teaching examples: reuse the moves, never the claims.

## Pass 1 · Term winnability

**The message-queue save.** The prompt-queueing feature was nearly announced as "message queue" — a term owned for forty years by Kafka/RabbitMQ answers. The winnability argument ("every engine answers 'what is a message queue?' with computer science; 'prompt queueing' is greenfield") reached the naming conversation before launch. When the product name was briefly "message queue" anyway, the fallback was entity-scoping: the definitional H2 became "What is the message queue **in Bolt.new**?" — disambiguation instead of a hopeless head-on fight.

**Coinage tiering.** "Vibe coding" had the search volume (citation rank #179, a gap worth chasing); "bespoke basics" was our invention with zero external volume. Verdict: the volume term headlines the piece, the coinage lives inside it as an H2. Citations chase the term people type; the campaign keeps its language.

## Pass 2 · Question tree

Tracked prompts became H2s nearly verbatim (the prompt set then lived in Profound; it lives in Cairrot now, and the technique is identical): "How do you build a SaaS app without hiring developers?" and "Can AI app builders replace traditional no-code platforms like Bubble?" went from tracked queries to headings. The searcher's phrasing IS the heading; a clever rephrase breaks the match.

## Pass 3 · Answer-first

Before (buried): "Sky's story starts with a golf store in trouble..." then costs in paragraph four.
After: H2 "What does it cost to run a small e-commerce business on SaaS?" opens "For Scratch Golf, as of 2026, the answer was about $600 a month across 10 or so subscriptions..." — the extractable answer first, the story below it.

## Pass 4 · The definitional block

From the prompt-queueing launch post, four signals in one cluster:
> "Prompt queueing lets you send prompts to Bolt.new, an AI app builder, while it's still working on the last one. ... It's live in every Bolt.new project as of August 2026."
Term + entity-with-category + claim + date. An engine lifting this carries the attribution with it.

## Pass 5 · Receipts

- **Origin, not middleman:** the campaign's Zylo conflict (36% vs 40% unused licenses in different assets) was settled by fetching Zylo's own 2026 SaaS Management Index: 36%. The 40% was a stale intermediate figure. Aggregator numbers ("Discussion Mode saves 90%") get excluded when the origin doc doesn't carry them.
- **Honest scoping:** Clutch's $132,480 average is real but enterprise-skewed; "custom software used to cost $132,480" is the trap, "hiring a development shop, where projects average $132,480" is the truth.
- **Consent as a receipts problem:** "one photographer building his print store" is not anonymized — it's identifiable to one person. Genuine anonymization survives a motivated guess: "one owner we interviewed."
- **Cross-page consistency:** one live page said Sky saved ~$600/month; a second said $3K/month. The fix was scoping language ("software **and services**") — but the finding is the point: check the campaign's other live pages for the same numbers.

## Pass 6 · Liftable formats

The controls table from the launch post did double duty — highest-citability format AND settled a permissions ambiguity that three sibling assets got wrong in prose:

| Control | How | Applies to |
|---|---|---|
| Edit | Open it, rewrite, confirm | Your own prompts |
| Reorder | Drag prompts up or down the line | Any prompt in the queue |

The "Applies to" column answers the follow-up question before it's asked. Build columns like that.

## Pass 7 · FAQ

The problem-piece FAQ used the searcher's own words as questions ("Why did my credits go so fast?") with 40–60 word single-intent answers. Anti-duplication in practice: the listicle's FAQ asked "Do I own what I build?"; the Sky deep dive's FAQ deliberately asked adjacent questions (cost to run, replace all at once?) instead of repeating it.

## Pass 8 · Freshness

"Launched this week" was caught pre-publish: true on the draft date, false on any later publish slot. Became "launched in August 2026" — dated claims must survive slot changes. Same pass added the docs links (support.bolt.new) as trust-completeness.

## Pass 9 · The glaze

Launch-eve, two behaviors unconfirmed (does the queue auto-pause on errors? does drag work mid-stream?). The glaze: rewrite to what was demo-certain — "The failed prompt keeps its retry button, and the queue stays under your control: stop the line, fix or retry the failed step... and resume" — true whether auto-pause shipped or not. The stronger sentences ("The queue pauses itself...") were banked in a Copydesk comment anchored to the passage (or, without Copydesk, a marked note in the draft), making the post-confirmation upgrade a paste. The piece shipped honest on a deadline that couldn't wait for engineering.
